Eco-Smart Station (RainErgy)

  • Academic project
  • Team thesis, 5 co-authors
  • Submitted for patent or utility model registration

A Computer Engineering thesis prototype that collects rainwater, filters it, dispenses it, and provides device charging, powered by a hybrid solar and microturbine system and monitored by an ESP32.

Problem
Improving accessibility of water and device charging using harvested rainwater and renewable energy.
My role
Co-author on a team of five. I developed the ESP32 hardware–software integration for water-level monitoring and automated chlorine dosing.
Built with
ESP32, sensors, pumps, multi-stage filtration, solar and microturbine power, automated controls

Implemented in the prototype

  • Rainwater collected into separate storage drums
  • Multi-stage filtration unit
  • Hybrid solar and microturbine power
  • Coin-operated water dispensing and device charging
  • No-cost emergency water access
  • ESP32 water-level monitoring with automated chlorine dosing

Tested (10 trials, controlled setting)

  • Water filtration: 100%
  • Energy generation consistency: 80%
  • Coin interface, dispensing, charging, chlorine dosing, water-level monitoring, emergency access: 90% each
  • Rated “Very Much Acceptable” by 10 experts and 136 end-users (grand means 4.64 and 4.72)

Planned (future work)

  • Laboratory water-quality testing
  • Outdoor deployment
  • Cashless payment

Laboratory water-quality testing is still listed as future work, so no claim is made that the treated water is safe to drink.
