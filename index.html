Development and Implementation of Eco-Smart Station: Rainwater Harvesting and Hybrid Energy Solutions for Water and Charging Accessibility. University of Rizal System, College of Engineering, Antipolo City.

My roleAutomatic chlorine doser, local app

My contribution

Automatic chlorine doser (local app)

I developed the local application that reads input from the water level sensor and controls the automatic chlorine dosing. It was my main technical responsibility in the station's monitoring and dosing system.

What it is

An ESP32-based prototype that harvests rainwater, filters it through a multi-stage unit, and generates hybrid solar and microturbine power. It also offers coin-operated water dispensing, device charging, and emergency water access for the community.

The EcoSmart web app

The web app I built for the thesis: live monitoring, chlorine dosing control, analytics, and a built-in doser manual.

How we built it

A Developmental Research Design guided by the ADDIE model, with repeated build, test, and refine cycles.

  1. 1AnalysisIdentified the need for water and charging access in the community.
  2. 2DesignPlanned the hardware modules and how the control logic connects them.
  3. 3DevelopmentBuilt the modules and wrote the local control app for dosing and monitoring.
  4. 4ImplementationAssembled the modules into one integrated station.
  5. 5EvaluationTested each module, then refined the design through repeated cycles.

Team

Five co-authors: John Marlon Amador Aungon, Althea Lizette Rodriguez Palustre, Daron Miguel Sigua, Shan Wesley Bachain, and Vincent Bryant Lovedioro Yuson. For more on how the work was split, contact Shan or ask the AI.
